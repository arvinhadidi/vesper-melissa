The celtic cross is the big, ten-card spread most people picture when they imagine a proper tarot reading. It is not mystical machinery. It is a structured way to look at one question from several angles, including the ones you would rather skip. Use it for questions that deserve a long, slow look.

## The ten positions

Lay the cards in this order:

1. **The heart of the matter.** Where you are right now.
2. **The crossing card.** What challenges or complicates it. Place it sideways across card one.
3. **The foundation.** What sits beneath it: the root cause or history.
4. **The recent past.** What is just leaving.
5. **The crown.** What you hope for, or the best realistic outcome.
6. **The near future.** What is coming next.
7. **You.** How you see yourself in this.
8. **Your surroundings.** The people and conditions around you.
9. **Hopes and fears.** Often the same thing in disguise.
10. **The outcome.** Where things head if the current pattern continues.

Cards one to six form a cross in the middle. Cards seven to ten stack in a column on the right, with ten at the top.

## How to lay it out

Shuffle, ask your question, and place the first card down. Cover it with the second one sideways. Then place the third below, the fourth to the left, the fifth above, the sixth to the right. Build the column last, bottom to top.

Read the cards as they come, one position at a time. Say what you see before interpreting. A question that suits this spread is something like "What do I need to understand about leaving my flatshare and moving in with my partner?" A quick yes/no does not need ten cards, so try [yes or no tarot](/guides/yes-or-no-tarot) instead.

## Read it as a story, not a list

Beginners often read ten separate meanings and end up with ten disconnected sentences. Instead, read in pairs and clusters:

- **1 and 2:** the situation and its snag.
- **3 and 4:** where it came from and what is passing.
- **5 and 6:** the hope against what is actually next. A gap between these two is useful.
- **7 and 9:** you and your hopes and fears. Do they match? If card seven is [Strength](/cards/strength) and nine is [The Moon](/cards/the-moon), you appear confident but are afraid of what is unseen.
- **8:** the context. Is it supportive or crowded?
- **10:** the destination, read against card five.

Check the cards' suits across the spread. A cross full of swords tells you the issue is mostly about thinking and conflict. Lots of cups means feelings are at the centre. Many major arcana cards suggest a big theme rather than a small hiccup.

## A short worked example

Question: "Should I move cities for my partner's job?"

1. [Two of Pentacles](/cards/two-of-pentacles): you are juggling it all.
2. [Five of Wands](/cards/five-of-wands): a lot of competing opinions.
3. [The Hierophant](/cards/the-hierophant): the expectations you were raised with.
4. [Three of Cups](/cards/three-of-cups): friends you will miss.
5. [The Star](/cards/the-star): you want hope and a fresh start.
6. [Ace of Pentacles](/cards/ace-of-pentacles): a concrete opportunity is arriving.
7. [The Hermit](/cards/the-hermit): you are thinking alone, not talking.
8. [Seven of Swords](/cards/seven-of-swords): someone is holding back information.
9. [The Moon](/cards/the-moon): fear of the unknown.
10. [The Sun](/cards/the-sun): an open, warm result.

The story: you are tired of juggling, the opinions are loud, and there is an actual chance available. The block is the silence, so before deciding, ask who is not telling you everything, and talk to your friends. The outcome is good if you choose it on purpose, not by default.

## Common mistakes

- Using ten cards for a question that has a simple answer.
- Fixating on the outcome card. It is a direction, not a prophecy.
- Skipping the crossing card because it feels uncomfortable. It is often the most useful one.
- Changing the position meanings halfway through.
- Reading it when you are exhausted. Do a smaller [three card spread](/guides/three-card-spread) instead.

## Make it a habit, not an event

The celtic cross is best used once in a while, not weekly. Write down the question, the cards and your instinctive reading, then revisit after a month. You will see what held up and what did not. A private journal makes this easy, and [Melissa can walk you through a spread](/onboarding) and save it for you.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is the celtic cross too hard for beginners?

Not if you read it in pairs rather than as ten separate meanings. It helps to know a handful of cards first. Try a [three card spread](/guides/three-card-spread) for a few weeks, then move up to ten when you feel steady.

### What is the crossing card in a celtic cross?

The second card, laid sideways over the first. It shows the main challenge or complication in your question, sometimes an outside force and sometimes your own habit. It is often the most helpful card in the layout, because it names what needs attention.

### How long does a celtic cross reading take?

For yourself, expect twenty to forty minutes if you read it carefully and write notes. Rushing defeats the point. Lay the cards, describe each, link them in pairs and write a short summary at the end.
