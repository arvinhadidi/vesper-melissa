Learning to read tarot is less about memorising 78 meanings and more about learning to look at a picture and say something honest about it. You do not need a special gift, a perfect deck or a candle collection. You need a deck, a question and about ten minutes a day.

## How the deck is built

A standard deck has 78 cards in two groups.

**The 22 Major Arcana** are the big themes: [The Fool](/cards/the-fool) at the start, [The Lovers](/cards/the-lovers), [Death](/cards/death), [The Tower](/cards/the-tower), and [The World](/cards/the-world) at the end. When several majors turn up, the situation feels weighty.

**The 56 Minor Arcana** are everyday life, split into four suits of fourteen cards (ace to ten, plus four court cards):

- **Cups** cover feelings, love and relationships. See the [Ace of Cups](/cards/ace-of-cups).
- **Swords** cover thoughts, conflict and communication. See the [Three of Swords](/cards/three-of-swords).
- **Wands** cover drive, creativity and work. See the [Ace of Wands](/cards/ace-of-wands).
- **Pentacles** cover money, health, home and routine. See the [Ten of Pentacles](/cards/ten-of-pentacles).

**Court cards** (page, knight, queen, king) can be people in your life, or ways you are behaving. A [Knight of Swords](/cards/knight-of-swords) might be the friend who fires off texts at midnight, or you doing the same.

The full set is listed on the [card meanings page](/cards).

## Choosing a deck

Start with the Rider-Waite-Smith deck or a close copy. Almost every book and website uses its imagery, and every card, even the pips, has a scene. Avoid decks with only symbols and no pictures, as they are harder to learn from. Choose one you like looking at, because you will spend time with it.

## Shuffling and asking

Shuffle however you like: overhand, riffle, or spreading them across the table. Stop when it feels done. Ask a clear, open question about your own situation, such as "What should I focus on this week?" Avoid questions that need a mind-reader. You can read more about question wording in the [love tarot reading guide](/guides/love-tarot-reading) and the [yes or no tarot guide](/guides/yes-or-no-tarot).

## Reading reversals

A reversed card is simply upside down. Many readers use reversals, but you can ignore them for your first month. When you add them, read a reversed card as one of these: the energy is blocked, it is happening internally, it is excessive, or it is on its way out. Do not treat it as the opposite. A reversed [Strength](/cards/strength) might mean self-doubt, not weakness.

## Learn the cards without memorising

Flashcards are dull and rarely stick. Try these instead:

1. **Describe the picture first.** What is the person doing? What is in the background? Is it day or night?
2. **Use the suit and number.** Cups plus three is a gathering of friends. Swords plus three is heartbreak. Numbers carry meaning: aces are beginnings, fives are conflict, tens are completion.
3. **Link to a story.** The Hermit holds a lantern on a mountain. That is you ignoring your phone for an evening.
4. **Look up the meaning after your own guess.** Not before.

## Your first spreads

Start with one card a day, as in our [daily tarot reading guide](/guides/daily-tarot-reading). After a week, move on to the [three card spread](/guides/three-card-spread). Leave the [Celtic Cross](/guides/celtic-cross) for later, when you have a feel for the deck.

## A seven-day practice plan

- **Day 1:** Look through the whole deck slowly. Sort the cards into four suits and the majors. No reading.
- **Day 2:** Pull one card. Describe it in three sentences without looking anything up.
- **Day 3:** Pull a card and write down where it showed up in your day.
- **Day 4:** Study the four aces. Compare them.
- **Day 5:** Pull a card with a specific question about work or study.
- **Day 6:** Try a three card spread: situation, obstacle, advice.
- **Day 7:** Read back your notes. Which cards confused you? Which made sense? Choose three to study next week.

A notebook or a private journal helps, and [Melissa can pull and save your cards](/onboarding) while you learn. She also lets you carry on the conversation about a reading, which is handy when a card makes no sense.

## Habits that help

Keep readings short and honest. Do not read for the same question repeatedly. Treat a card as a prompt for reflection, not as a decision. Take a break if you find yourself pulling cards at 2am for reassurance.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a gift to read tarot?

No. Tarot reading is a skill based on observation, pattern recognition and reflection. Anyone can learn it with regular practice. The cards are prompts for your own thinking, so what matters is your willingness to look honestly at the picture and the question.

### Which tarot deck is best for beginners?

The Rider-Waite-Smith deck is the usual recommendation, because every card has a full picture and most guides refer to it. Pick a version whose artwork you like. Avoid decks without scenes on the minor cards, which are harder to read intuitively.

### How long does it take to learn tarot?

You can read a simple spread within a week or two. Feeling fluent usually takes a few months of regular practice, and learning continues for years. Pull one card daily, journal it, and the meanings begin to stick through repetition rather than memorisation.
