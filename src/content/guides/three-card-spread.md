The three card tarot spread is the one most readers return to for life. It is quick, flexible and forgiving: three cards, one short story, and room to see how they talk to each other. If you only learn one layout, learn this one.

## How to lay it out

Shuffle while holding your question in mind, then draw three cards and place them left to right. Turn them over one at a time. Before you look anything up, say what you see in each picture. Then read them as a single sentence, not three separate verdicts.

The beauty of this spread is that the positions are yours to choose. Decide them before you draw, not after.

## Past, present, future

The classic. Card one is what led here, card two is where you stand, and card three is where things are heading if nothing changes.

Treat the third card as a direction of travel, not a promise. If it is not what you hoped for, ask what you could change now. The spread is good for general check-ins, such as a Sunday-night "where am I with all this?" You can combine it with a [daily tarot reading](/guides/daily-tarot-reading) to build a habit.

## Alternative position sets

Past, present and future is not always the right fit. Try these:

- **Situation, obstacle, advice.** Best for a problem with a clear edge, such as a job offer or a stubborn argument.
- **You, them, the relationship.** Good for love questions, and it keeps the focus on what is observable. See the [love tarot reading guide](/guides/love-tarot-reading) for more.
- **Mind, body, spirit.** A gentle check-in for when you feel off but cannot say why. The "body" card often points at something practical, like sleep or the lunch you skipped.
- **What to keep, what to drop, what to try.** A tidy spread for decisions and clear-outs.
- **Head, heart, action.** For when you are torn. The head card is the logic, the heart card is the feeling, and the action card is what to do next.

## A worked example

Question: "I have been seeing someone for six weeks and I cannot tell if I am more into it than he is. What should I know?" Position set: you, them, the relationship.

**You: [The Empress](/cards/the-empress).** A woman in a cushioned field with a crown of stars and a heart-shaped shield. You are giving generously: planning, remembering, offering. This is lovely, and it is also a lot of effort to carry on your own.

**Them: [Two of Swords](/cards/two-of-swords).** A blindfolded figure holds two crossed blades with a calm sea behind. He is holding his guard up and not committing, either way. Notice that the card describes him avoiding a choice, not rejecting you.

**The relationship: [Six of Cups](/cards/six-of-cups).** Two children exchanging a cup of flowers. There is sweetness and nostalgia here, but also a sense of staying in the safe, early stage.

Read together: you are giving fully, he is hesitating, and the connection is still in its pleasant opening chapter. The reading does not say he is wrong for you. It suggests you ask for clarity rather than keep giving more. A sensible next step: say what you want, and notice how he responds.

For a pending decision, a [yes or no tarot](/guides/yes-or-no-tarot) pull can add a quick lean.

## How to read the three together

Check these things:

1. **Suits.** Are all three cups (feelings), swords (thoughts), wands (action) or pentacles (practical matters), like the [Ace of Wands](/cards/ace-of-wands) or the [Four of Pentacles](/cards/four-of-pentacles)? A suit imbalance tells you where the energy sits.
2. **Majors and minors.** Major Arcana cards such as [The Moon](/cards/the-moon) signal bigger themes. Minor cards are day-to-day.
3. **Direction.** Do the figures face each other, or look away? The Rider-Waite pictures are full of clues.
4. **Story.** Say it as a sentence: "Because of X, I am in Y, which could lead to Z."

## Common slip-ups

- Reading each card in isolation. They belong together.
- Treating the future card as fate.
- Changing the position meanings after seeing the cards.
- Pulling a fourth card because you did not like the third. Pulling a clarifier is fine if you decide it in advance, but not as a reflex.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best three card tarot spread?

Past, present and future is the most common, but the best spread is the one that fits your question. Situation, obstacle and advice works for problems, and you, them and the relationship suits love questions. Choose the positions before you draw.

### How do I read three tarot cards together?

Read them as one sentence rather than three separate meanings. Look at the suits, whether major or minor cards dominate and which way the figures face. Then tell the story: because of the first card, you are in the second, which could lead to the third.

### Can I use a three card spread for a daily reading?

Yes, though one card is usually enough for a day. A three card pull works well at the weekend as a weekly check-in. Use morning, afternoon and evening as positions, or what to notice, what to avoid and what to try.
