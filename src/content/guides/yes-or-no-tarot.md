Yes or no tarot is the fastest way to get a straight answer from a deck, and also the easiest way to fool yourself. Used well, it tells you which way you are leaning before you have admitted it. Used badly, it becomes pulling cards until one says what you wanted at 1am.

## How yes or no tarot works

Tarot was not built as a yes/no system, so we borrow from traditional meanings. Cards about movement, success and open doors lean yes. Cards about delay, loss or closed doors lean no. Cards that are about choice, waiting or mixed motives lean maybe.

The answer is a lean, not a ruling. Think of it as a weather report for the question, not a court verdict.

## How to phrase the question

A yes/no question needs three things: it is about you, it has a clear action, and it has a time frame.

- Weak: "Will he text me?"
- Better: "Is it a good idea for me to message him this week?"
- Weak: "Should I quit?"
- Better: "Is handing in my notice before the end of the month the right move for me?"

Avoid questions that rely on another person's private mind, and avoid "or" questions. If you are choosing between two flats, ask about each separately. For wider love questions, our [love tarot reading guide](/guides/love-tarot-reading) has better formats.

## One card or three?

**One card** is clean and quick. Shuffle, ask, draw. Read the lean from the lists below and notice your immediate reaction. Relief or a sinking stomach tells you something too.

**Three cards** give you nuance. Read them as one sentence:

1. The first card is the answer's lean.
2. The second is what pushes against it.
3. The third is what happens if you act.

A [three card spread](/guides/three-card-spread) works nicely here. If two cards lean yes and one leans no, the answer is "yes, but watch that one thing".

## Cards that lean maybe

- [The Moon](/cards/the-moon), because something is unclear or unsaid.
- [The Hanged Man](/cards/the-hanged-man), because you are in a pause.
- [Two of Swords](/cards/two-of-swords), because you are refusing to look at it.
- [Wheel of Fortune](/cards/wheel-of-fortune), because the situation is still moving.
- [Seven of Cups](/cards/seven-of-cups), because you have too many fantasies and not enough facts.

A quick example: you ask whether to accept the flatshare with your friend's friend. You draw the Eight of Cups, a figure walking away from stacked cups at night. That leans no, and it often means a feeling of leaving something half-done. You might ask: what am I walking away from if I say yes?

## What about reversals?

Reversals soften or flip the lean, but only if you use them. A reversed yes card often means "yes, later" or "yes, with conditions". A reversed no card can mean the worst is passing. If you are a beginner, ignore reversals for yes/no and keep it simple. Our [beginner guide](/guides/how-to-read-tarot-for-beginners) explains when to add them.

## When not to use yes or no

Skip it when:

- The decision is medical, legal or financial. Ask a professional, then use tarot to reflect.
- You are asking about someone else's feelings. A card cannot read his mind.
- You have already asked three times. The answer has not changed, and neither has your anxiety.
- You are in a spiral. Ask an open question instead, such as "What do I need to see about this?"

Yes or no is a tool for clarity. If you notice you are using it to avoid deciding, put the cards down and make a list of pros and cons on actual paper. ## Frequently asked questions

### Is yes or no tarot accurate?

Tarot does not predict fixed outcomes, so think of it as reflection rather than certainty. Yes or no tarot works best for showing which way you are leaning. If the card lands badly, that reaction is useful information about what you actually want.

### Can I ask the same yes or no question twice?

You can, but it rarely helps. A second pull tends to reflect your mood rather than new information. If you must, wait a few days, or rephrase the question to explore a different angle, such as what is holding you back.

### What does it mean if I pull a maybe card?

Maybe cards usually mean the situation is unclear, still moving or missing information. Ask what you do not know yet, and what you could do this week to find out. A maybe is often a request for one more honest conversation or a bit of patience.
