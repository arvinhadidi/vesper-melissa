There is a half-filled spreadsheet of rents on one tab and a draft message to your landlord on another. Moving is exciting and a bit terrifying, and everyone around you has an opinion. Tarot will not pick a postcode for you, but it can show what you are moving towards and what you are running from, and those are rarely the same.

## How to ask it

"Should I move?" is a weak question because the honest answer depends on a dozen things a card cannot know: rent, visas, your job, who you would be leaving. A raw yes or no also hides the real tension, which is usually between wanting a change and fearing a loss. These work better:

- What am I hoping a new place will fix, and will it follow me?
- What would I miss most, and what would I be glad to leave?
- What does staying one more year offer, and what does it cost?

Write your answers beside the cards afterwards. You will often find the reading only confirmed something you wrote in the first minute.

## A three-card spread for it

Try these three positions:

1. **Staying.** What life looks like if you do not move.
2. **Going.** What the move would honestly bring, including the awkward parts.
3. **What you need to decide.** The thing you must check, ask or sort out first.

## Cards that lean go

**[Six of Swords](/cards/six-of-swords)** shows a ferryman poling a boat with a woman and child across calm water. A move that leaves trouble behind and heads for quieter shores. The swords in the boat are your baggage, so you carry it with you, but the water is smooth.

**[The Fool](/cards/the-fool)** steps off a cliff edge with a small bag and a white rose, a dog at his heels. It is the leap. It does not promise safe landing, but it does say the trust you feel is real.

**[Eight of Wands](/cards/eight-of-wands)** is eight staves flying through open sky. Things are about to move quickly: viewings, offers, deadlines. If it appears, expect momentum, and be ready to book the van.

## Cards that lean stay

**[Four of Pentacles](/cards/four-of-pentacles)** shows a figure gripping a coin, with more under his feet. Security matters, and it can also mean you are holding on to something that is not as stuck as you think. Ask which is true for you.

**[Four of Swords](/cards/four-of-swords)** is a knight lying at rest in a church, three swords on the wall. You need recovery, not a house hunt. If you are exhausted, a move may only add chaos.

**[Five of Pentacles](/cards/five-of-pentacles)** has two figures trudging through snow past a lit window. Money or support may be thin. Check your finances and your people before you commit to a new city.

## Cards that lean wait

**[Two of Wands](/cards/two-of-wands)** shows a figure on a battlement holding a globe. You are weighing the world and have not picked a direction. Keep researching.

**[Two of Pentacles](/cards/two-of-pentacles)** is a figure juggling two coins, waves rolling behind. You are managing two options at once, and it is fine to keep balancing for now.

**[The Hermit](/cards/the-hermit)** holds a lantern on a snowy peak. Go quiet, think, and decide away from other people's opinions. Your lantern lights only the next step, and that is enough.

For a quick tie-break between two options, see [yes or no tarot](/guides/yes-or-no-tarot). If your question is really about a person, [Is he the one?](/questions/is-he-the-one) may be what you are circling.

## What to do with the answer

Move the cards into real life. A lean go card is a cue to price it out: rent, deposit, commute, the date you would need to give notice. A lean stay card is a cue to ask what would make where you are better, and whether you could try that first. A lean wait card is permission to set a deadline, perhaps a month, and to visit before deciding. Whatever you draw, speak to one person who knows your circumstances before you sign anything. Cards are good for gut checks, but a tenancy agreement is not.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can tarot tell me whether to move cities or countries?

It cannot tell you what will happen, and it should not replace practical checks like visas, contracts and budgets. What it does well is bring up how you feel about leaving and staying, which is often what is really stopping you.

### What if the cards say stay but I want to go?

Notice that. A reading can reveal what you want when you feel disappointed by it. Ask what the stay card is warning about, and see whether you can solve that rather than abandon the plan.

### Does the Fool always mean I should move?

Not at all. It means the leap is real, and it asks whether you have packed what you need first. A leap with a plan is courage. A leap with none is just a fall.
