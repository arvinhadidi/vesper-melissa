It is late, you have reopened the chat, and you are checking whether he has watched your story again. You want a straight answer to one question, and nobody you know can give it. Tarot cannot tell you what he will do, but it can tell you a lot about what you are holding on to, and that is the part you can actually work with.

## How to ask it

"Will my ex come back?" is a weak question because it hands all the power to him. The cards cannot read another person's decision, and a yes would only keep you waiting by the phone. A no would not stop you hoping either. Better questions point back at you:

- What do I need to understand about why this ended?
- What am I hoping he will give me, and can I give it to myself?
- What would reconnecting need from both of us, and is that realistic?

Pick one and write it down before you shuffle.

If this relationship ever left you feeling unsafe, watched or controlled, that is not a tarot question. Please talk to someone you trust about it, a friend, family member or your GP, before you think about contact.

## A three-card spread for it

Draw three and lay them left to right:

1. **What ended.** Look at the plain facts the card shows, not the story you tell at 2am.
2. **What is still open.** Look for unfinished feeling, an unsent message, something unsaid.
3. **Where this goes if you do nothing.** Treat this as a direction of travel, then ask what you would change.

## Cards that usually mean yes

**[Judgement](/cards/judgement)** shows an angel's trumpet and figures rising from their coffins, arms open. For an ex it is a call to reckon honestly with the past, and it often appears when a real second conversation is possible.

**[Six of Cups](/cards/six-of-cups)** is a child handing another a cup of flowers in a sunny courtyard. It points to old warmth and shared history. It can mean reunion or plain nostalgia, so ask whether you miss him or just being looked after.

**[Two of Cups](/cards/two-of-cups)** shows two people exchanging cups under a winged lion's head. It is the card of mutual interest. If it lands in the "them" position, the pull may well be two-way.

## Cards that usually mean no

**[Eight of Cups](/cards/eight-of-cups)** shows a figure walking away from stacked cups at night, towards the hills. Someone has decided it is not enough, and it may be you.

**[Death](/cards/death)** has a skeleton on a white horse and a sun rising between two towers. It is an ending that clears space, not a punishment. Here it usually says this chapter is closed.

**[Five of Cups](/cards/five-of-cups)** is a cloaked figure staring at three spilled cups, with two still standing behind them. Grief is real, but you are looking at what has gone and missing what is left.

## Cards that usually mean wait

**[The Hanged Man](/cards/the-hanged-man)** hangs calmly upside down with a halo. It means pause and change your angle. Nothing is decided yet, and pushing will not help.

**[Two of Swords](/cards/two-of-swords)** is a blindfolded woman holding two crossed blades by a still sea. Someone is avoiding a decision, and it can be him or it can be you.

**[The Moon](/cards/the-moon)** has a dog and wolf howling under a pale moon. Things are unclear and feelings are loud. Do not act on what you think you see tonight.

## What to do with the answer

A yes card does not mean he will text. Use it to ask whether you want him back or want the version of him you remember. A no card does not mean you are stuck. It can be permission to stop checking his socials. A wait card is the most useful: it tells you to stop spending the week on this.

Whatever you draw, write down one small action for tomorrow that has nothing to do with him: a walk, a call to a friend, an early night.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can tarot tell me if my ex will text me?

No. Tarot cannot read another person's mind or schedule, and anyone who promises a date and time is guessing. What it can do is show where your own feelings and expectations sit right now, which helps you decide whether to reach out first.

### Why do I keep pulling Death or the Tower when I ask about him?

Because those cards are about endings and sudden change, and a break-up is both. They rarely mean something terrible is coming. They usually mean the old pattern is over, and space is opening for something different, with or without him.

### How often should I ask the same question?

Once, then leave it. Asking again and again until you get a card you like only teaches you to ignore the ones you do not. Give it a week, and ask a different question, such as what you need to heal.
